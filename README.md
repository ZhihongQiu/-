The gravitomagnetic effect is the cause of the flat shapes of galaxies. It needs to be analyzed using gravitoelectromagnetism.

Two current-carrying coils, under the action of a magnetic field, will flip until they are parallel to each other.

An orbiting planet is equivalent to a “gravitational ring current,” which generates a gravitomagnetic field. Under gravitomagnetic interactions, the orbits of different planets will gradually flip until they become mutually parallel (The decisive factor is the gravitomagnetic force between stellar spin and planetary orbital motion). Combined with the constraint from gravitational attraction that the orbits share a common center, the orbits of celestial bodies will gradually become coplanar, ultimately making galaxies appear flat. 

This effect can replace the traditional “initial angular momentum + angular momentum conservation pancake-flattening” model. Numerical simulations based on the traditional model often produce only ellipsoidal or spherical structures and cannot well explain the observed flat galaxy morphology.
